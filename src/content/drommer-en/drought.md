## What does it mean to dream of drought?

The ground is cracked in a pattern like old porcelain. The riverbed is there, but the water is
not. Everything looks as it did, only drier, and nothing is going to happen until something
changes from outside.

Drought is the only dream in the water family about absence. All the others concern too much,
too deep or too fast. This one concerns nothing.

## Absence as a symbol in itself

### What has stopped coming

Drought does not threaten. It simply continues. That makes it a precise image for periods when
nothing goes wrong but nothing grows either — a job without development, a relationship without
movement, a stretch when the days resemble one another.

### The landscape that remembers water

What is characteristic about drought dreams is that the structures are still there. The
riverbed, the well, the channel. Everything meant to carry water exists — it is simply empty.
Many describe precisely that as the worst part of the dream.

### When it is raining somewhere else

Several people notice that there is water in the dream, just not where they are. That detail is
often the most important, and it points towards comparison rather than towards scarcity itself.

## What the drought did to the landscape

**If the ground was cracked:**
The classic image. The cracks show that it has lasted, and the dream tends to emphasise the
duration.

**If plants or crops were withering:**
Something you have tended is not surviving. Often described in periods when effort yields no
result.

**If a [river](/en/dreams/river/) or well had run dry:**
A source that has given before. That is a different feeling from a place that was always dry.

**If it began to rain towards the end:**
A common and strikingly positive turn. Many remember that moment better than the rest of the
dream.

**If you searched for water without finding it:**
The most exhausting variant. The searching is usually the whole content, not what you failed to
find.

## When the dream comes in stagnation

The drought dream rarely arrives in a crisis. It comes more often in periods when nothing is
happening: after a long stretch without change, in a phase of waiting for something that does
not come. It is a dream about depletion rather than about danger.

The dream sits close to the [desert](/en/dreams/desert/), but differs from it. The desert is a
place that has always been dry. Drought strikes a place that used to have water, and that
difference carries the meaning.

## Sources and further reading

- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to the dream arriving in periods without movement rather than in acute crises.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on water as a life-giving symbol, relevant to
  what its absence does to the image.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on how sustained states find
  different images from acute events.
