## What does it mean to dream of a swimming pool?

You are standing at the edge, or you are already in. The water is blue and calm, the
bottom is visible, and it is marked where the deep end starts. Nothing about this water
is unpredictable.

That is precisely why the pool differs from the other water dreams. The sea and the river
have wills of their own. The pool does not — someone decided both its shape and its depth
in advance.

## Water with edges

### The controlled format

Where the [ocean](/en/dreams/ocean/) is feeling you have no say over, the pool is feeling
in a format somebody set. That can be calming, and it can be confining. Which of the two
the dream emphasises usually says the most.

### The bottom you can see

Most water dreams concern what you cannot see. The pool inverts that: here the bottom is
visible the whole way. Dreams of pools are therefore described as frightening less often
than dreams of open water.

### A place where others see you

The pool is almost always a public place. It is marked out, it has rules, and there are
usually people there. Many of these dreams are more about the others round the edge than
about the water.

## What happened in the pool

**If the water was drained:**
An empty pool is one of the more unsettling images in this family. The form is there, but
the content is missing.

**If you moved from the shallow to the deep end:**
A clear transition. Often described in periods when someone is moving from something safe
towards something they cannot yet stand up in.

**If others were swimming and you stayed put:**
More often concerns belonging than water. The edge is a good image for being present
without taking part.

**If the water was cloudy or green:**
A pool that should be clean but is not carries extra weight. Sits close to dreams of
[murky water](/en/dreams/murky-water/).

**If you jumped in:**
Many remember the jump itself better than the rest. Going from edge to water is a decision
the dream likes to dwell on.

## When the pool is one you know

Some people dream of a very specific pool from childhood. The place is then as important
as the water, and the dream borrows a whole period of life through that one room.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of [swimming](/en/dreams/swimming/),
where the movement is the theme. Here it is the frame round the water that carries the
meaning.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on water as an image of the
  unconscious, relevant to what it does to that image when the water is given edges.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to familiar places from one's own life dominating dream settings.
- Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, *The Content Analysis of Dreams* (1966) —
  normative data on how often other people are present in dreams.
