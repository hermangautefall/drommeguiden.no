## What does it mean to dream of a waterfall?

You hear it before you see it. Then you are standing there, and the volume of water is
more than you can take in. Waterfalls are among the few natural phenomena that make as
strong an impression in a dream as they do awake.

Where the [river](/en/dreams/river/) concerns direction and current, the waterfall
concerns something else: the point where water stops flowing and starts falling. It is a
transition that goes one way only.

## The force that cannot be held back

### What finally gives

The waterfall is a precise image for something that has built up and is now yielding. Many
dream it in periods when something long held back — anger, grief, a decision — is on its
way out.

### The fall that goes one way

Water that has gone over the edge does not come back up. Dreams of waterfalls often carry
that irreversibility: something is done, and it cannot be undone. Whether that feels like
relief or loss varies.

### The sound people remember

Strikingly many remember the sound better than the sight. A waterfall drowns out everything
else, and the dream readily uses that to say something about a situation where one thing is
taking up all the space.

## Where you stood in relation to it

**If you saw it from a distance:**
The most common variant, and the gentlest. Distance allows you to observe something in
progress without being in it.

**If you stood directly beneath it:**
The force hits you head on. Often described in periods when something overwhelming comes
from a direction you cannot move away from.

**If you went over:**
A rarer and more dramatic variant. It shares a great deal with dreams of falling, with
water's element added.

**If the waterfall was dry:**
A striking image. A fall without water is often described in periods when something that
used to have force no longer does.

**If you went behind it:**
The space behind a waterfall is a motif in its own right. Finding a dry place in the middle
of something overwhelming tends to be read as a form of shelter.

## The waterfall in Nordic landscape

In Norway the waterfall is a landscape feature people actually have a relationship with,
not only a symbol. That makes the dream more concrete here than in many places — many
people dream of one particular fall they have stood beside.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a [river](/en/dreams/river/), where
the current and the direction carry it. Here it is the fall.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on water as an image of the unconscious,
  relevant to why a fall in water is read as something irrevocable.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to the dream arriving when something held back is giving way.
- Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, *The Content Analysis of Dreams* (1966) — normative
  data, relevant because it shows how much space natural settings occupy in dreams.
