## What does it mean to dream about killing someone?

Few dreams stir up as much shame and confusion as this one. You wake up, remember what you dreamed, and the first thought that comes is often: what kind of person am I? Let's start with what matters most: dreaming about killing someone does not make you a dangerous, evil, or disturbed person. This is a dream a great many people have had — and it almost always turns out to be about something entirely different from what it looks like on the surface.

These dreams are the subconscious's most dramatic language for expressing something that otherwise finds no outlet. They deserve to be understood, not feared.

## What the killing in the dream is really aimed at

### Suppressed anger and frustration

The most common interpretation of dreams about killing someone has to do with anger — feelings you haven't been allowed to express, frustration you've swallowed, or a situation where you've had to hold back something that really wanted out.

The person you kill in the dream doesn't have to be someone you're actually angry at. The subconscious often chooses a stand-in — a figure who symbolises the source of the frustration — rather than staging a direct confrontation. You might dream of killing a stranger, an acquaintance, or even someone you love, without it saying anything about your real feelings toward them.

The question the dream is asking is: what are you angry about? And have you let yourself feel it?

### A wish to put an end to something

In dream symbolism, killing someone is often an image for wanting to end something — not a person, but what that person represents. A relationship, a situation, a role, a part of yourself. The person in the dream might symbolise the job you want to leave, the relationship you're considering ending, or an old version of yourself you no longer want to be.

"Killing" this figure is the subconscious's way of processing a wish for a definitive ending — something that in real life can feel difficult to even admit wanting.

### An inner conflict

Sometimes the person you kill isn't an outside figure at all, but a part of yourself. An inner conflict between two sides — the cautious self and the bold one, the dutiful self and the free one, who you are and who you want to be — can play out as a violent encounter in the space of the dream.

Jung called this meeting with the Shadow: the sides of ourselves we don't acknowledge, which nonetheless insist on being heard. "Killing" them in the dream isn't a victory — it's a sign that the conflict is still unresolved.

### Control and power in a situation where you lack it

Dreams about killing — related to dreams of [being chased](/en/dreams/being-chased/) — can also arise during periods when you feel powerless, when something or someone has control over your life in ways you don't like. The dream gives you an arena where you regain control, even if the means are drastic. It isn't a wish for actual violence — it's an expression of frustration at feeling powerless.

## The victim, the feeling and what they tell you

**Who you killed:**
This is the most important clue. Did you know the person? What do they represent in your life? A [boss](/en/dreams/boss/) can symbolise authority, an ex can symbolise a closed chapter, a [stranger's](/en/dreams/stranger/) face can represent an unfamiliar side of yourself.

**How you felt while it happened:**
Anxiety and reluctance point toward an inner conflict. Relief or calm points toward a wish for closure. Indifference might mirror something that's already over emotionally, even if it hasn't formally ended.

**If it was self-defence:**
You're reacting to something that threatens you — not attack, but defence. Something in your life is making you feel unsafe, and you're fighting back.

**If you felt regret afterward:**
An inner conflict about something you want to do but aren't sure you should — or an action you've already taken that still sits with you.

**If you felt nothing at all:**
This can mirror an emotional distance from someone or something in your life, or a numbness that might be worth paying attention to.

## Putting an end to something — symbolic violence

It's worth saying clearly: dreaming about killing someone almost never reflects an actual wish to harm anyone. This is a very common dream, and the mind reaches for an extreme, dramatic image simply because it's effective — not because it means anything literal.

The most common reading has to do with figuratively "putting to death" something: ending a habit, retiring a role, closing out a relationship, or letting an old version of yourself die so something new can take its place. The victim in the dream often represents exactly what you, deep down, want to be rid of — not an actual person.

For others, the dream can be a safe outlet for anger or frustration that has no room in waking life, or it can be about regaining control and power in a situation where you feel powerless. The guilt that so often follows the dream is almost always a sign that you're a thoughtful person — not evidence of anything else.
