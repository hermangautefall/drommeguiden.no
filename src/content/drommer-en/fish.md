## What does it mean to dream of fish?

They move below you, or beside you, or in a tank you are standing and looking into. Fish in
dreams are rarely frightening, and they seldom do anything dramatic. People remember them
anyway.

The fish differs from other animals in dreams on one point: it lives somewhere you cannot see
into. Every other animal shares a space with you. The fish does not.

## What exists below the surface

### The life you do not see

Water is often read as the unconscious, and the fish then becomes what actually lives down
there. Nothing threatening — simply something that exists and moves without your being aware of
it. That may be the most precise reading of this symbol.

### Bringing something up

Fishing is one of the few motifs where you bring something from the depths to the surface.
Dreams of catching fish are often described in periods when something has gone from being a
hunch to being something you can look at.

### Abundance and what gives itself

In many cultures the fish stands for plenty — the sea gives without anyone sowing. That
symbolism is old, and it underlies many dreams of shoals and full nets.

## What the fish did

**If the fish swam calmly:**
The most common variant, and the gentlest. Often described as peaceful rather than meaningful.

**If you caught one:**
Something from the depths has come up. What you did with it afterwards is then the most
important detail.

**If the fish was dead or lying on land:**
A strikingly strong image. A fish out of water is something in the wrong element, and it is
often read that way.

**If there were many fish at once:**
The shoal is a motif of its own. It is tied to abundance more often than to being overwhelmed,
unlike most other dreams of quantity.

**If the fish was unusually large or brightly coloured:**
Many remember this best. The deviating feature usually carries more than the species.

## The fish as a symbol through time

The fish is one of the oldest symbols we have, and it has carried very different meanings:
abundance in Nordic coastal culture, spiritual insight in several religious traditions,
fertility in others. That breadth means the context of your own dream matters more than the
tradition.

The dream sits close to articles on individual species such as [salmon](/en/dreams/salmon/) and
[goldfish](/en/dreams/goldfish/), but differs from them in that here it is the fish as such —
what lives below the surface — that carries the meaning.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the fish as an archetypal image of content
  from the unconscious, and on water as its element.
- Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, *The Content Analysis of Dreams* (1966) — normative data
  on which animals actually appear in dreams.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to fish being commoner in the dreams of people with a relationship to the sea.
