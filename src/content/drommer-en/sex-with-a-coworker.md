## What does it mean to dream about sex with a coworker?

It's one of the dreams people are most embarrassed to admit to — and one of the most common sexual dreams there is. You dream about having sex with a coworker, maybe even your [boss](/en/dreams/boss/), and wake up with an uncomfortable mix of confusion and shame.

Take a breath. It doesn't mean you're attracted to them. It doesn't mean you want a relationship. It almost always means something else entirely.

## Admiration and power dynamics

### Admiration for something the coworker represents

The sexual imagery in dreams is one of the mind's strongest expressions of attraction — and attraction can be about far more than sex. Admiring a coworker's competence, confidence, creativity, leadership, or social ease can surface as a sexual dream.

Ask yourself: what does this coworker represent for you? What do you see in them that you want more of in your own life or work? That's probably what the dream is actually expressing.

### A wish for closer, more genuine contact

Working life is full of roles, facades, and expectations. Many people spend more hours with coworkers than with people they'd call friends — and yet the connection often stays surface-level and transactional.

A sexual dream about a coworker can mirror a wish for real connection with them — to see and be seen, to communicate more openly, to build an actual human relationship underneath the roles. The sexual is the dream's strongest image for that kind of intimacy.

### Power dynamics and hierarchy

Dreams about sex with a superior are especially common — and especially confusing. But they're rarely about desire. They're about power.

Sexuality in the dream can symbolise a wish for more influence, recognition, or autonomy at work. Being intimate with someone who has power over you can represent a wish to reclaim some of that power — to be treated as an equal, to be seen as a peer, to gain more control over your own working life.

### Work-related stress and anxiety

During periods of pressure, conflict, or insecurity at work, the brain processes this through dreams. Sexual dreams about coworkers can arise in exactly these periods — not as an expression of desire, but as the mind's attempt to resolve tension and build connection in a context where relationships feel strained.

## What the coworker represents

**If it was a superior or boss:**
Power dynamics and a wish for recognition are the likely theme. What do you want from them, professionally?

**If it was a peer you get along with:**
Probably a wish for closer contact — the dream's sexuality is the mind's exaggeration of a social wish.

**If you don't actually like the coworker:**
Especially interesting. What do they represent for you anyway? A quality you fear, or envy?

**If the dream repeated itself:**
Something unresolved in the relationship, or in your relationship to working life more broadly.

**If the dream caused discomfort at work the next day:**
Normal and expected — but remember that dreams aren't chosen. You're not responsible for what your mind does while you sleep.

## The workplace as a social landscape

Sexual dreams about coworkers are far more common than most people assume, and they tend to arrive during periods of real pressure at work. What they're rarely about is worth noting: actual attraction. Far more often, they're about relationship and power — closeness, rivalry, recognition, and position within a group you spend most of your day with.

Jung would likely have read the coworker as a shadow figure, or an anima or animus figure — an externalisation of a quality the psyche wants to integrate. The sexual encounter, in that reading, is the union of two sides, not two bodies. No cultural tradition treats dreams like this as an omen of real events or hidden wishes; they're consistently understood as processing dreams tied to social standing and hierarchy.
