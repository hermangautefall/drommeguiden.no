## What does it mean to dream of a snake attacking?

It lies still, and then it is moving. Snake dreams are unpleasant in themselves,
but it is the attack that makes people remember this particular one. Many wake at
the moment it strikes.

The snake as a symbol concerns transformation, venom and healing. The snake that
*attacks* concerns something else: the moment when something unexpected went from
lying still to reaching you.

## The attack that came without warning

### What you did not see coming

Threat simulation theory proposes that dreaming rehearses dangerous situations in
a safe format. The snake is a good choice for that rehearsal — it is fast, hidden
and hard to anticipate. One reading is that the dream is rehearsing precisely the
experience of being caught out.

### The bite that had a location

Where other snake dreams are diffuse, the attack is precise. It happens at a
moment, at a place on the body, and it hurts. That precision is usually the most
usable thing the dream gives you.

### When the danger was known in advance

Some see the snake long before it strikes. The dream then changes character: it
concerns surprise less, and more a threat you have been walking around aware of
without acting on.

## How the attack unfolded

**If the snake bit your hand:**
Hands are what we act with. Can point to something that struck a decision or a
piece of work rather than you as a person.

**If it bit your foot or leg:**
Sits close to the theme of forward motion. Often described in periods when
something halted a direction you were taking.

**If it struck but missed:**
Common, and milder than it feels. A warning that did not become harm tends to be
read as a fear that has not yet materialised.

**If it attacked someone else:**
Shifts the centre of gravity from you to a relationship. Who it concerned is then
the most important piece of information.

**If you managed to stop it:**
Described by many as a turn in the whole dream. Moving from exposed to acting is
worth noting, whatever the snake stood for.

## When the dream returns

If the attack repeats, and always in the same way, it is worth looking for
something in waking life with the same shape: something that lies still for a
while, and then demands everything of you at once.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of
[snakes](/en/dreams/snake/) in general, where the animal's double nature is the
starting point. It shares more with [being chased](/en/dreams/being-chased/) —
both concern being exposed to something moving towards you.

## Sources and further reading

- Antti Revonsuo, "The reinterpretation of dreams" (2000) — threat simulation
  theory, relevant because an animal attack is exactly the situation the theory
  holds that dreaming rehearses.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the snake as an archetypal
  image, relevant to why the attack feels larger than the animal.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the dream arriving after something that landed
  unprepared.
