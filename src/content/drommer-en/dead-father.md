## What does it mean to dream of your late father?

He is sitting in his chair, or standing in the doorway, or walking ahead of you
without turning round. Often he says nothing. Yet something is to be decided in the
dream, and you feel it without it being said.

Dreams of a late father have a different character from dreams of a late mother.
Where the mother often carries care, the father more often carries judgement — and
that means the dream arrives in different situations.

## The gaze that still measures

### The one who set the measure

For many, the father is the first person who appraised performance. Whatever the
relationship was like, he therefore easily becomes an image of the question "am I
doing this right?". The dream fetches him when that question is live again.

### Decisions that call him up

The dream arrives strikingly often around choices: changing jobs, moving, becoming
a father yourself, taking something over. Not because he would have had an opinion,
but because the situation of deciding activates the whole notion of being seen from
outside.

### Silence as a hallmark

Many notice that the father in the dream says little. That is common, and it is
often more telling than words would have been. The absence of a verdict can be read
as validly as a verdict.

## What he did in the dream

**If he was approving:**
Can mirror your having settled something yourself, rather than his actually having
said it. The approval in the dream is usually phrased by you.

**If he was critical or disappointed:**
More often concerns your own doubt than him. The criticism is yours, and the dream
has only given it a voice you recognise.

**If he walked ahead of you without turning round:**
A recurring variant. Often described in periods when someone feels they should have
asked for advice they can no longer get.

**If you did something practical together:**
Driving, repairing, working. Many remember these as the warmest, and they tend to
concern a companionship that needed no words.

**If he was younger than when he died:**
Common. The dream often fetches a father from a period when the relationship was
simpler, not from the end.

## When the unresolved presents itself

If the relationship was difficult, the dream often comes more frequently than for
those who had an easy one — not less. What was left unsettled has more work
remaining, and grief after a complicated relationship is not smaller, only harder
to put into words.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a
[deceased person](/en/dreams/deceased-person/) in general. It shares a theme with
dreams about [your boss](/en/dreams/boss/) — both concern being appraised by
someone who has held authority over you.

If you are carrying grief that is hard to bear, talk to someone you trust or reach
out to a helpline where you live.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the father figure as an
  archetype, relevant to why he carries judgement rather than care.
- J. William Worden — the tasks of mourning as a model, relevant to why a
  complicated relationship leaves more work, not less.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the dream arriving around decisions.
