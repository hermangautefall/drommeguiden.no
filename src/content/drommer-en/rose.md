## What does it mean to dream about a rose?

The rose is one of human culture's most loaded flowers — a symbol of [love](/en/dreams/love/), beauty, secrecy, and the sacred across thousands of years. In dreams, it carries this same duality: one of the most beautiful things there is, but with thorns that will hurt you if you're not careful.

## Beauty with thorns

### Love, and beauty that costs something

The primary reading of the rose is love — but not the easy, trouble-free kind. The rose is a reminder that what's genuinely beautiful and valuable almost always has something sharp attached to it. A good relationship costs vulnerability. Meaningful work costs effort. A deep friendship costs honesty.

To dream of a rose is to touch this paradox: beauty carried forward by something rough and sharp.

### Vulnerability and the courage to bloom

A rose opening is one of the most powerful images there is for vulnerability and the courage to be seen. The bud that stays closed is protected — but it never blooms. In dream symbolism, the rose can raise the question of whether you dare to open up: in a relationship, in a creative project, in life generally.

### Romance and longing

Sometimes the reading is simpler and more direct: a longing for romance, beauty, and meaning. A stretch where daily life has felt too grey, too practical, too far removed from what makes life worth living.

### Secrets and the sacred

"Sub rosa" — under the rose — is an old expression for the confidential, the secret. A white rose hung over a meeting table once signalled that what was said there would stay hidden. The rose is therefore also a symbol of the inner, private life — what you keep for yourself.

## The rose's colour and condition

**If the rose was red:** Love, passion, and deep emotional attachment.

**If the rose was white:** Purity, peace, and something sacred or untouched.

**If the rose was yellow:** Friendship, joy, and connection without romantic charge.

**If you were pricked by a thorn:** The beauty you're after asks something of you. Are you willing to carry it?

**If the rose was wilting:** Something beautiful is at risk of being lost. What needs care and water?

**If you gave someone a rose:** A wish to give of yourself — of the most beautiful thing you have.

## The rose as a symbol of love

The rose is Western culture's foremost symbol of love — Aphrodite, Venus, and the Virgin Mary all carry it. In Celtic tradition, the rose is tied to the five elements through its five petals. In Islamic mysticism, the rose is an image of God's love for creation.

Freud read the rose sexually, as he did most flowers. Jung saw it as one of the archetypal images of the mandala's wholeness — the rose blooming at the centre as the self coming into being.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — the rose as a mandala-like image of the Self: beauty with thorns, wholeness with its own defences.
- Artemidorus of Daldis, *Oneirocritica* (c. 150–200 AD) — flowers as dream motifs in antiquity, read by their condition and colour.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why the state of the rose tends to mirror the dreamer's own.
