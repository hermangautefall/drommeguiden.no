## What does it mean to dream of dead relatives?

They are gathered round a table. Your grandmother, an uncle, perhaps someone you
only know from photographs. They are talking to one another, and you are there
without anyone finding it strange. The dream is rarely frightening — more often it
is oddly safe.

This is a different dream from the one about a single dead person. When several are
gathered, they stop being individuals and become something else: a family, an
origin, a place you come from.

## The gathering as a motif in itself

### From person to generation

One dead person in a dream carries a relationship. Several carry a line. That is
why this dream is rarely about missing one particular person — it is about
belonging to something that existed before you.

### The periods that call it up

The dream tends to arrive when questions of belonging become live: moving, illness,
having a child, becoming the oldest in the family. It is no coincidence that the
generations before you appear then.

### The house that is almost always there

The gathering rarely happens somewhere neutral. It is usually a
[childhood home](/en/dreams/childhood-home/), a kitchen, a sitting room — a room
where the family actually did gather once. The place does much of the work in this
dream.

## Who was present

**If most were from the same generation:**
Tends to point to a particular era in the family rather than to the people. Often it
is the time when you yourself were a child.

**If some living people were there too:**
Entirely common. Dreams do not separate the living from the dead the way waking
thought does, and it means nothing about any of them.

**If someone you expected to see was missing:**
Often the most telling detail. The absence tends to say more about an unresolved
relationship than the whole of the rest of the dream.

**If nobody spoke to you:**
Common, and not a rejection. Many describe themselves as onlookers in this dream,
which fits its being about belonging rather than conversation.

**If the mood was good:**
The most common variant. The dream of gathered dead is strikingly seldom
unpleasant, unlike most other dreams about death.

## When the dream recurs across years

Some people have this dream regularly throughout life, always with the same
assembly. That is common, and it is more often tied to a stable sense of where one
comes from than to unresolved grief.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a single
[deceased person](/en/dreams/deceased-person/), where the bond to that one human
being is the whole content. It has more in common with dreams of a
[grandmother](/en/dreams/grandmother/) — both concern the generation before you.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the collective unconscious and
  archetypal notions of origin, relevant to why the family appears as a whole.
- The Icelandic family sagas (written down c. 1200–1350) — dream accounts in which
  dead relatives appear gathered, read as cultural history.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the dream arriving when belonging is a theme in waking
  life.
