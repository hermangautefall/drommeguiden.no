## What does it mean to dream of talking to someone who has died?

You are sitting opposite one another, and they say something. Nothing grand —
perhaps just that they are all right, or an answer to something you had wondered
about. You remember the voice when you wake, and that is the detail people most
often recount afterwards.

Of all dreams about the dead, this is the one most often described as good. Many
call it a visit, and that is worth taking seriously as an experience even though we
cannot say what it is.

## The conversation that stands apart

### Why words in particular

In most grief dreams the dead person is present without speaking. When the words
come, it tends to be because something is actually to be conveyed — and it is
usually something you have been carrying yourself. The dream gives it a sender you
trust.

### The words that are yours

Everything said in a dream is phrased by you. That sounds like an objection, but it
is not. That you managed to give the comfort or the permission a voice you recognise
is itself work that has been done.

### What you managed to say

Many put most weight on what they got to hear. Often it matters more what they got
to say. Thank you, sorry, an objection never voiced — that is where the dream does
the most.

## What was said

**If they said everything is all right:**
The most common sentence in this kind of dream. It is described as relieving, and it
often comes at a stage when grief is finding a form.

**If you got an answer to something specific:**
Can mirror your having reached a conclusion without registering it. The answer in
the dream is then a confirmation, not information.

**If you argued, or it turned uncomfortable:**
It happens, and it does not mean anything is wrong. Unresolved relationships are not
resolved by someone dying, and the dream takes up what is there.

**If you could not say what you wanted to:**
A familiar variant, and often the hardest. It usually points to something that still
needs room, not to a lost opportunity.

**If the conversation was completely ordinary:**
The gentlest form, and for many the finest. Talking about nothing is what people
miss most.

## When the dream comes once and never again

Many have this dream a single time, often early in grief, and remember it for years.
That is common. That it does not recur does not mean something was closed too soon.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a
[deceased person](/en/dreams/deceased-person/) in general, where the presence is the
whole content. Here it is the exchange that carries it.

If you are carrying grief that is hard to bear, talk to someone you trust or reach
out to a helpline where you live.

## Sources and further reading

- J. William Worden — the tasks of mourning as a model, relevant to why a
  conversation in a dream can do real work.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on emotional
  processing, relevant to why this variant in particular is remembered so long.
- The Icelandic family sagas (written down c. 1200–1350) — dream conversations with
  the dead as a motif in Norse tradition, read as cultural history.
