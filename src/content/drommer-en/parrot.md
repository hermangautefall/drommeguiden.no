## What does it mean to dream about a parrot?

Are you speaking with your own voice in the dream, or just repeating something you heard somewhere else? A parrot is an animal that talks without always understanding what it's saying — it imitates, repeats, and reflects, not unlike the symbolism in dreams about a [mirror](/en/dreams/mirror/). Dreaming about a parrot raises exactly this question of authenticity.

The tone of the dream shapes a lot of the answer.

## Between echo and your own voice

### Imitation and a lack of originality

The most classic reading of the parrot is about imitation — either you're repeating something without having thought it through, or someone in your life is echoing you or others without any real substance behind it. The dream might be asking: is this actually what you mean, or is it something you've picked up from someone else?

### Communication and the spoken word

The parrot talks — and speech itself is central to the dream's message. It can be about something you said that had bigger consequences than you expected, something you should say but haven't, or something that's being said about you.

### Colour and joy

The parrot is vivid and full of life — and in dreams where it's beautiful and delightful, the message can be about joy, creativity, and expressing yourself freely.

## Noise, silence, and who was speaking

If the parrot repeated something you yourself had said, it's often a warning that your words live on longer than you think — pay attention to what you communicate. If the parrot was noisy and irritating, it can mean that something in your environment feels superficial and loud, and that some quiet might be overdue. If the parrot was beautiful and quiet instead, the dream is about creative expression and beauty — an encouragement to show more of yourself. If you were the parrot yourself in the dream, it can mean you've taken on other people's opinions or behaviour without quite noticing.

## The voice that repeats someone else's words

In many cultures, the parrot is a symbol of intelligence and communication — but also of the superficial and the merely learned. Psychologically, the parrot can represent the parts of us that have absorbed other people's voices — parents, society, authority — without ever putting them through a critical filter of our own.

The dream can invite you to ask: what's actually my own voice? If the parrot keeps turning up in your dreams, it might be worth writing it down in a dream journal — over time, patterns tend to say more than any single dream does on its own.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — the persona that repeats other people's words instead of finding its own.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why dreams like this arrive when your own voice feels distant.
