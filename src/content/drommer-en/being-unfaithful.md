## What does it mean to dream that you are unfaithful?

You wake and remember it at once. You did something in the dream you would never
do, and the guilt is there before you can remind yourself that nothing happened.
Some people carry it for days.

This dream is far more common than people think, and it is almost never a hidden
wish. It uses a strong image for something that is usually a good deal more
ordinary.

## The guilt that came first

### The act the dream chose

Infidelity is perhaps the clearest image we have for breaking an agreement. If the
dream is to show you that you are giving something attention at the relationship's
expense, there are few sharper ways to do it. Work, a project, a friend, an
interest — any of these can lie behind it.

### When it is about you, not the relationship

Many describe the dream in periods when they are dissatisfied with themselves
rather than with their partner. The guilt is already there, and the dream looks
for an act to attach it to. The infidelity then becomes an image rather than a
content.

### When something genuinely is unresolved

Sometimes the connection is more direct. If there is an attraction you have not
admitted, or a distance in the relationship you have not spoken about, the dream
can point straight at it. The mark of this is usually that it recurs and that the
person stays the same.

## Who you were unfaithful with

**If it was a stranger:**
The most common variant. It points more often to something undefined you are
missing than to a person, and rarely concerns attraction.

**If it was [your ex](/en/dreams/ex/):**
Usually concerns a period of your life rather than the person. The ex is often an
image of who you were back then.

**If it was someone you know well:**
The most uncomfortable, and the one that most often causes embarrassment.
Closeness and confidence use the same imagery as attraction in dreams.

**If you were found out:**
Then the fear of consequences is the core, not the act. Many recognise that from
entirely different parts of life.

**If you felt no guilt in the dream:**
Worth noticing. The absence of guilt is often described in periods when someone
feels hemmed in by obligations generally.

## When it comes back again and again

If it comes again and again, it is worth looking at what in the relationship has
gone unsaid. The dream often has a simple trigger: a conversation being put off,
an imbalance in who gives most, a sense of not being seen.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of
[infidelity](/en/dreams/infidelity/) in general, where the partner's betrayal is
usually the starting point. Here you are the one acting, and that turns the whole
reading round: this is a dream about you.

## Sources and further reading

- Sigmund Freud, *The Interpretation of Dreams* (1900) — on embarrassment dreams
  and how dreaming stages what we otherwise hold back.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the dream arriving when something in the relationship or
  in your own life is unresolved.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on emotional
  processing, relevant to why the guilt lingers so long after waking.
