## What does it mean to dream of your late partner?

You are sitting having a meal. He asks about something practical, or she laughs at
something on television, and nothing in the dream marks that anything is wrong. It
is precisely the ordinariness that makes waking so heavy.

Dreams of a late partner differ from other grief dreams. A parent is often an image
of something — care, judgement, origin. A partner rarely is. There the person is
usually simply themselves, and that is why the dream lands so precisely.

## The ordinary that carries the loss

### What you did without thinking about it

Few people dream of the wedding day or of great occasions. They dream of breakfast,
of the drive, of a conversation about nothing. That is where life actually took
place, and that is where the absence is felt most clearly.

### The partner who knew your days

Nobody else knew what your Tuesdays looked like. Losing that is losing a witness,
not only a person, and dreams of a late partner often concern exactly that lost
matter-of-factness.

### When the dream changes over time

Many describe the dreams of the early period as restless and searching, and the
later ones as calm and ordinary. That progression is common, and it usually follows
grief's own course rather than anything you can steer.

## How the dream unfolded

**If everything was as it had been:**
The most common form, and for many the hardest to wake from. It does not mean you
have suppressed anything.

**If you said goodbye:**
Often described as the most meaningful. Many experience it as something they were
given rather than something they invented.

**If they wanted to say something and could not get it out:**
Can mirror something unresolved. It may as easily be yours as theirs.

**If you were with someone else in the dream:**
Common, and not a betrayal. The dream often works on the question of moving on long
before you ask it consciously.

**If you could not find them:**
Searching is a familiar motif early in grief. It usually eases of its own accord.

## When the dream becomes rarer

Many are unsettled when the dreams tail off, as though the bond were coming loose.
It usually is not. Grief's task is not to break the bond but to find a form in which
it can be carried on — and fewer dreams often means that form is being found.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a
[deceased person](/en/dreams/deceased-person/) in general. It also differs from
dreams of [loneliness](/en/dreams/loneliness/), although the two often come in the
same period.

If you are carrying grief that is hard to bear, talk to someone you trust or reach
out to a helpline where you live.

## Sources and further reading

- J. William Worden — the tasks of mourning as a model, relevant to why the bond is
  to find a new form rather than be broken.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on emotional
  processing, relevant to why the dreams change character over time.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the ordinary rather than the ceremonial dominating the
  dream.
