## What does it mean to dream about darkness?

Darkness isn't absence — it's the presence of something not yet known. In dream symbolism, darkness is one of the key images for the unconscious: the vast, the full, what isn't visible in the direct light of awareness but is nonetheless there, alive and active.

## The unknown and the uncontrolled

### The unconscious in its purest form

Jung consistently used darkness as a metaphor for the unconscious — not as something evil or dangerous, but as everything not yet made conscious. It's the storeroom of everything we don't yet know about ourselves: suppressed feelings, undiscovered resources, forgotten wisdom, future possibilities that haven't taken shape yet.

To dream of darkness is to stand in contact with that storeroom. The question isn't whether the darkness is dangerous — the question is whether you dare stand in it long enough to start seeing.

### Fear of the uncontrolled

For many, darkness is uncomfortable because it's unpredictable and can't be controlled. You don't know what's there. You can't plan. Darkness in a dream can mirror a similar experience in your waking life: something unpredictable, something that can't be controlled, something uncertain in a way that's hard to sit with.

### The darkness of beginnings

In creation stories across cultures, the world begins in darkness. Darkness isn't the end — it's the beginning. It's the state where things haven't yet found their form, but are on their way.

Darkness in a dream can mirror that you're in such a state: something is taking shape but isn't visible yet. It's not a problem to solve. It's a stage to stand in.

### Grief, depression, and an inner winter

Darkness is also an image of grief. During periods of depression, sorrow, or an inner winter landscape, darkness appears as the dream's natural backdrop. It's not a diagnosis — it's the psyche's honest description of an inner state.

## Whether the darkness was threatening or safe

**If the darkness was threatening:** Fear of the unknown is active. Something in your life feels unpredictable, and it's generating anxiety.

**If the darkness was quiet and calm:** There's room for stillness in darkness — not all unknowns are threatening.

**If there was light within the darkness:** An insight or a path is starting to appear. Something is becoming visible.

**If you wandered in the dark without finding your way:** A period of confusion and lost direction.

**If the darkness was hiding something:** Something in the unconscious wants attention — something that hasn't yet come to light.

## The darkness before the beginning

Darkness as sacred is a recurring theme across religious history. Apophatic theology, the idea that God exceeds all human understanding and is therefore best described through darkness, is one of the oldest strands of Christian mysticism. In Norse mythology, the realm of Hel is dark and quiet — but not exclusively a place of punishment. It's also a place of rest, for those who didn't fall in battle. Darkness isn't only threat — it also holds the sleeping, the grieving, and those waiting for something new.

## Sources and further reading

- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — how the psyche gives the unknown its strongest images.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why darkness feels threatening or safe depending on where you stand.
