## What does it mean to dream you cannot scream?

Something dangerous is happening and you draw breath to call out. Your mouth
opens. A thin sound comes, or nothing at all. You try again, harder, and the
result is the same.

The dream is among the most widespread there is, and it often leaves a strange
after-feeling in the throat on waking. It probably has two layers: one that sits
in the body, and one that sits in what you are carrying.

## The voice that will not come out

### The paralysis that covers the voice too

During REM sleep the skeletal muscles are disconnected, and the muscles that
control the voice are part of that. The brain can issue the order to shout without
anything happening. The dream takes in the missing sound and makes it part of the
action.

### Why it feels so strong

Calling out is one of the most instinctive things we do when frightened. That
precisely this reaction drops away produces a helplessness few other dreams reach
as accurately. That is probably why most people remember this dream well.

### The word you did not get to say

Alongside the explanation there is a content. The dream often comes in periods
where something is unsaid — an objection, a boundary, a truth that costs something
to say aloud.

## Who you were calling to

**If nobody nearby reacted:**
Tends to point to a sense of not being heard, in a relationship or a place where
you have tried to speak up.

**If you were calling to someone you know well:**
Can mirror something unresolved right there. Who it was is often the most usable
piece of information the dream gives you.

**If you were trying to warn someone:**
Often concerns responsibility. The feeling of seeing something coming without
being able to stop it is familiar from many waking situations.

**If the sound came, but far too faintly:**
A common variant. Can be read as having spoken up, but without the weight you
wish you had carried.

**If you woke from your own shout:**
Happens when the atonia releases at the moment the attempt is made. It is
unpleasant, but it says nothing more about the dream than that it reached all the
way to waking.

## When it comes with other dreams

Some recognise the same situation each time, with the same place and the same face
that does not turn round. Others get it only in periods when much is unresolved.
Both are common, and the difference says more about what the dream connects to in
you than about the dream itself.

The dream often appears in company with related images: [falling](/en/dreams/falling/),
[drowning](/en/dreams/drowning/), being unable to move. They share the same core —
a body that does not do what it should at the moment it matters most.

If it comes often, and always with the same face on the other side, it is worth
taking as an occasion to consider what is actually going unsaid.

## Sources and further reading

- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on nightmares
  and emotional processing, relevant to why the helplessness in this dream stays
  so long after waking.
- Matthew Walker, *Why We Sleep* (2017) — on REM sleep and the muscular paralysis
  that covers the voice as well.
- Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, *The Content Analysis of Dreams* (1966) —
  normative data, relevant because it shows how much space failed actions occupy
  in ordinary dreams.
