## What does it mean to dream about a rainbow?

A rainbow is one of nature's most striking and rare phenomena — and it always appears at the border between [rain](/en/dreams/rain/) and [sun](/en/dreams/sun/), between storm and clarity. It's no accident that in nearly every culture, it stands for hope, reconciliation, and a bridge between worlds.

Dreaming of a rainbow is rarely a negative sign. It's one of the dream world's brighter, more uplifting symbols.

## Common rainbow dreams and what they mean

### Hope and brightening after hardship

The most common reading is about hope — a sign that something difficult is starting to lift, that things are brightening after a dark stretch. A rainbow always follows the rain, and in dreams it functions as a kind of promise: it was hard, but there's something beautiful on the way.

### Passage and a bridge between worlds

In Norse mythology, the rainbow is Bifrost, the bridge between Midgard and Asgard. In dreams, it can represent a passage between states, phases, or worlds: you're moving from one place to another, and the road there is beautiful.

### Diversity and the richness of the spectrum

A rainbow holds every colour, and can represent diversity, complexity, and the richness of life's full range. A rainbow in a dream can be about seeing the whole picture, holding contrasts, recognising that life isn't black and white but layered and many-coloured.

### Where the arc stood, and whether you reached it

**If the rainbow was clear and full:**
A strong, positive sign — hope and passage are close and clearly visible.

**If the rainbow was faint or incomplete:**
Hope is there, but the situation isn't fully resolved yet. Something is on its way.

**If you walked toward the rainbow:**
You're actively pursuing what the hope represents — and that's a beautiful kind of movement.

**If there were two rainbows:**
A doubled promise, or an especially strong reassurance. Particularly charged.

**If the rainbow arched over water:**
Hope in your emotional life — a period of emotional clearing is on its way.

## Integration of opposites

In dream psychology, the rainbow represents an integration of opposites — every colour gathered into one harmonious arc. It's one of the strongest symbols there is for psychological wholeness and reconciliation between different parts of the self.

## The arc as promise and bridge

Bifrost, the rainbow bridge of Norse mythology, is beautiful but dangerous: only the gods and the worthy may cross it, and at Ragnarök it breaks under the weight of the giants. In Christian tradition, the rainbow is God's covenant with Noah after the flood — a promise that the destruction won't repeat itself. Whatever tradition it comes from, the rainbow tends to carry the same core message: it's okay. It gets better.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — the integration of opposites: every colour gathered into a single arc.
- Artemidorus of Daldis, *Oneirocritica* (c. 150–200 AD) — sky signs as omens in the earliest dream interpretation.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why the rainbow tends to arrive after a heavy stretch.
