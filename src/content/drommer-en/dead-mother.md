## What does it mean to dream of your late mother?

She is in the kitchen. She turns round, says something completely ordinary, and in
the dream you know she has died without that mattering. Then you wake, and the two
seconds before you remember again are the hardest of the day.

This is among the most common grief dreams there is, and it has a pattern of its
own. It does not resemble other dreams of the dead, because a mother rarely appears
only as a person.

## The first relationship, continuing

### The care you need now

The mother is the first person who looked after us, and she therefore easily
becomes an image of care itself. Many dream of her in periods when they are tired,
ill or alone with too much. The dream gives a brief version of being looked after.

### When you become the one who cares

The dream arrives strikingly often when someone has a first child, takes
responsibility for an ageing parent, or otherwise changes place in the generational
line. Becoming the one who gives care wakes the memory of the one who gave it to
you.

### What was never finished

Mother–child relationships usually have a long history, and few people get to
finish everything. If something is unresolved — gratitude, anger, questions you
never asked — the dream is often given the job of taking it up again.

## How she was in the dream

**If she was well, as before the illness:**
Very common, and for many the most consoling. The dream fetches her from before the
loss, not from the end.

**If she knew she had died:**
A distinct variant, often experienced as stronger. Many describe it as a
conversation they got to have rather than as a dream.

**If she did not speak:**
Entirely common, and not a sign of rejection. Presence without words is what many
remember best.

**If she was angry or unfamiliar:**
Can mirror something unresolved that was not settled when she died. It more often
concerns you than her.

**If she was simply there in the background:**
The gentlest form. Often described in periods when the grief has settled and the
bond has found a place.

## When the dream comes in waves

Many find the dream disappears for months and then returns around anniversaries,
birthdays or major transitions. That is not random, and it is not a setback.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a
[deceased person](/en/dreams/deceased-person/) in general, where the bond as such
is the theme. It also differs from dreaming of [your mother](/en/dreams/mother/)
while she is alive — there the relationship is still in motion.

If you are carrying grief that is hard to bear, talk to someone you trust or reach
out to a helpline where you live.

- J. William Worden — the tasks of mourning as a model, relevant to why the bond is
  not to be broken but to find a new form.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on emotional
  processing, relevant to why grief keeps working at night.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to the dream returning around anniversaries and transitions.
