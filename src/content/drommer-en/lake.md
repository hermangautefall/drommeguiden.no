## What does it mean to dream of a lake?

The water lies completely still. The forest on the far side stands twice over, once real
and once reflected. There are no waves, no current, nothing in a hurry. Many describe this
as one of the most peaceful dreams they have had.

The lake differs from the [ocean](/en/dreams/ocean/) on one decisive point: it does not
move. The sea has tides and a will. The lake has depth, but no speed.

## Depth without movement

### Stillness as the hallmark

What makes the lake dream distinctive is the absence of event. Nothing rises, nothing
withdraws. The dream therefore concerns something happening less often, and a state more
often — a period when things have settled.

### The mirror lying there

A glassy lake reflects everything above it. That means the dream is often as much about the
sky, the trees and yourself as about the water. Many remember the reflection better than
the water.

### What is nonetheless down there

Stillness does not mean emptiness. The lake is deep, and the dream knows it. A calm surface
over something you cannot see is one of the more precise images for a period when
everything seems fine without your having looked.

## What the lake did

**If the surface was perfectly glassy:**
The most common variant, and the most peaceful. Often described in periods when something
has finally settled.

**If something broke the surface:**
A fish, a stone, a hand. The break is usually the whole dream, and what came up is the
detail worth remembering.

**If the water was black or bottomless:**
Depth without visibility. Sits close to dreams of [murky water](/en/dreams/murky-water/),
but with calm instead of unease.

**If you rowed or swam out:**
Moving away from land on still water is often described as pleasant, unlike the same act on
the sea.

**If the lake was frozen:**
The dream then changes character and approaches [ice](/en/dreams/ice/) — something halted
rather than still.

## The lake as a place you know

Many people dream of one particular body of water they have been to. The place then carries
a whole period of life, and the dream is as much about that time as about the water.

The dream differs from the [fjord](/en/dreams/fjord/), which holds sea water in an enclosed
landscape. The lake has no way out — it is its own, and that is part of why it feels so
calm.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on water as an image of the unconscious,
  relevant to why a still surface over deep water is read as it is.
- Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, *The Content Analysis of Dreams* (1966) — normative
  data, relevant because it shows how much space natural settings occupy among dream scenes.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to the dream arriving in periods that are themselves calm.
