## What does it mean to dream of many snakes?

You see one, and then you see there are several. They are across the floor, in the
grass, in a room you just walked through. None of them is necessarily doing
anything. It is the number that makes the dream hard to shake off.

This is a different dream from the one about a single snake. Where the individual
snake carries transformation and danger, the quantity carries something more
ordinary: the sense that there is too much at once.

## Quantity as a symbol in itself

### When nothing on its own is large

Dreams of many of something — snakes, insects, rooms, doors — tend to arrive in
periods when no single matter is serious, but the sum is unmanageable. The number
is the translation of "too much", not of "dangerous".

### Why it became snakes

Quantity needs an animal that already carries unease. The snake is a natural
choice because it makes the dreamer alert without anything having to happen. A
cloud of butterflies would not have produced the same feeling.

### The movement that does not stop

Many describe the snakes moving constantly without getting anywhere. That is often
what remains afterwards — not fear of being bitten, but unease about something in
constant motion around you.

## Where the snakes were

**If they were on the floor of a room you know:**
Tends to point to something domestic or private — tasks, obligations, things that
have piled up where you were supposed to rest.

**If they were outdoors in nature:**
More often concerns something outside your control. Nature is a place where
quantity is normal, and the dream becomes milder.

**If they were in or under the bed:**
The most unsettling placement. The bed is the place for rest, and something
disturbing precisely there carries extra weight.

**If they formed a knot or a tangle:**
The image of something insoluble. Often described in periods when several matters
have grown together and can no longer be separated.

**If they ignored you:**
Common, and worth noting. That the quantity does not threaten points to being
overwhelmed rather than to danger.

## When too much arrives at once

The dream tends to arrive when the calendar is full, when several people want
something from you at once, or when many small things have been left too long. It
translates fairly directly.

The dream sits close to, but differs from, dreaming of a single
[snake](/en/dreams/snake/), where the animal's meaning is the whole point. It has
more in common with dreams of [ants](/en/dreams/ant/) — a quantity of something
small that together becomes large.

## Sources and further reading

- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity
  hypothesis, relevant to quantity dreams arriving in periods with many
  simultaneous demands.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on how feelings
  find precise images in dreams, relevant to why being overwhelmed becomes number.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the snake as an archetypal
  image, relevant to why it became this animal in particular.
