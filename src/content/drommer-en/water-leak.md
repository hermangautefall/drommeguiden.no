## What does it mean to dream of a water leak?

It is dripping from the ceiling, or running down a wall, or there is a puddle on the floor that
was not there yesterday. Nothing dramatic happens. But you know it will not stop by itself, and
that it is doing damage while you watch.

Where [flooding](/en/dreams/flood/) is being overwhelmed at scale, the leak is the opposite:
little water, long time. It is an entirely different feeling, and it points somewhere else.

## The small thing that does not stop

### Damage that happens slowly

A leak does not destroy by force but by duration. That makes it a precise image for things that
are not urgent enough to prioritise, but that worsen while they go unresolved.

### Water where it does not belong

Water in a [house](/en/dreams/house/) is always the wrong water. The dream readily uses the
house as an image of yourself, and a leak then becomes something getting into what should have
been sealed.

### What you knew about and left

Strikingly many people describe having already known about the leak within the dream. That may
be the most telling feature: this is rarely a surprise, and more often something that has been
allowed to continue.

## Where it was leaking

**If it dripped from the ceiling:**
Something coming from above and outside your control. Sits close to themes of pressure from
others or from circumstances.

**If it leaked from a pipe in the wall:**
Hidden damage. That variant is often described in periods when something is going on without
one quite knowing where.

**If there was water in the [basement](/en/dreams/basement/):**
The lowest part of the house, the part you do not use daily. Often tied to something old rather
than something ongoing.

**If you tried to mop up while it kept coming:**
A familiar and defeated variant. The image of treating symptoms instead of cause, and many
recognise it.

**If you found the source:**
The most encouraging variant. Finding where it is leaking from is often described in periods
when something has finally been explained.

## When the dream comes in periods of postponement

The dream tends to arrive when something has gone unresolved for a while — a conversation, a
bill, a health matter, an arrangement nobody takes in hand. It is rarely alarming and more often
irritating, which fits what it usually points at.

## Sources and further reading

- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to the dream arriving when something unresolved has been left over time.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on the house as an image of the psyche,
  relevant to why water inside the house is read differently from water outside.
- Ernest Hartmann, *The Nature and Functions of Dreaming* (2011) — on how sustained, low-grade
  unease finds its own images in dreams.
