## What does it mean to dream about a rat?

Few animals trigger quite as immediate a sense of disgust as a rat showing up in a dream — the small grey shape that moves along the edge of your vision and disappears before you can properly react. It carries connotations of decay, [illness](/en/dreams/illness/), betrayal, and whatever hides in the dark. Almost no one dreams about rats with any pleasure. These dreams tend to stick, and they nearly always carry a message worth paying attention to.

## Common rat dreams and what they mean

### A hidden threat, gnawing away

The most common reading involves something that's causing damage slowly and out of sight — like a rat inside the walls. A situation, a relationship, or a pattern that's quietly eating away at something valuable without you quite seeing it. The rat in your dream is the unconscious mind's image for exactly this: something is there, it's doing damage, and it needs to come into the light.

### Betrayal, dishonesty, and contempt

The rat is, in many cultures, a symbol of betrayal — someone who informs on others, who stabs you in the back, who can't be trusted. Dreaming about rats can mirror a fear of, or a growing recognition that, someone in your life isn't trustworthy.

### Fear of something spreading

Rats are historically tied to plague and contagion, and in dreams they can represent a fear that something negative is spreading: a conflict, a toxic dynamic, a negative thought pattern that keeps growing and taking up more room.

### Your own Shadow

Jung would likely have read the rat as a manifestation of the Shadow in one of its least comfortable forms — the aspects of the self we despise most, the impulses we'd rather not acknowledge. A rat in your dream can represent something in yourself that feels dirty or unworthy, and that needs confrontation rather than continued suppression.

### From one rat to an infestation

**A swarm of rats:**
A problem that has grown into an infestation — something that was once small and manageable has become too big to ignore.

**A rat in your own house:**
Something destructive is inside your inner world — inside your own sense of self. See the symbolism of the [house](/en/dreams/house/).

**A rat you defeated:**
You're confronting and putting a stop to something destructive — an active response to a problem.

**A large, threatening rat:**
A threat too significant to ignore — something that calls for direct action.

**A rat that got away:**
What you're afraid of is avoiding confrontation, but it hasn't disappeared — it's just hiding somewhere else.

## The Shadow, and what we'd rather not see

In Jungian psychology, the Shadow refers to the parts of ourselves we don't want to recognise — impulses, traits, and feelings we push down because they feel too shameful or unworthy to show. The rat fits unusually well as an image for exactly this, because it lives precisely where the Shadow does: inside the walls, under the floor, in the dark no one else looks into. Meeting it in a dream can be the unconscious asking for something unfinished to be confronted, rather than pushed further down.

## Between plague and survival

In much of Western tradition, the rat carries an almost entirely negative charge — from historical plague outbreaks to the sewer rat of horror films. In Chinese tradition, though, the rat is actually the first animal in the zodiac cycle, representing intelligence, resourcefulness, and adaptability. Across most Northern European folklore, the rat remains primarily a negative symbol — a pest, a sign of decay, something no one wants in the house.

## Sources and further reading

- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — the shadow: what we'd rather overlook, and which multiplies precisely because it's hidden.
- Artemidorus of Daldis, *Oneirocritica* (c. 150–200 AD) — vermin as a dream motif, read as something that wears away quietly.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why rat dreams arrive when something neglected is asking to be noticed.
