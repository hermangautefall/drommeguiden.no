## What does it mean to dream about a wound?

A wound is an opening in something that was meant to be closed — an injury that hasn't yet healed. In dreams, a wound is one of the most direct images the psyche has for what hurts, what isn't finished being processed, and what's still tender to the touch.

## What's open and unprocessed

### Something open and unprocessed

A wound that hasn't yet closed is a wound that's still bleeding. In the language of dreams, this is a direct image of something emotional or relational that isn't healed: a loss you haven't properly grieved, a hurt that hasn't been processed, a conflict that hasn't been resolved.

The wound in your dream asks a concrete question: what in your life is still open? What hurts when you touch it?

### A hurt that lives on

Wounds come from trauma, from blows, from something breaking through the skin. In the symbolic language of dreams, a wound often mirrors a violation — something that broke through your boundaries and left a mark. It might be words that were said, actions that were taken, broken trust, or loss.

Interestingly, wounds in dreams aren't necessarily new. An old, infected wound mirrors something that's had too little attention over time and has grown larger in the dark.

### Vulnerability made visible

To have a wound is to have a tender spot — a place where you're open and unprotected. The wound in your dream can mirror a feeling of being more vulnerable than you like to admit, or that something vulnerable about you has become visible to others in a way you didn't choose.

### The healing process is underway

Not every wound dream is about something open and bleeding. Dreams of wounds that heal, that close over, that are being tended — are positive images of an ongoing process of repair. The psyche is at work on something.

## Where the wound sat, and whether it healed

**If the wound was bleeding heavily:**
Something is draining your energy right now — an active, ongoing pain that demands attention.

**If the wound was old and infected:**
Something long neglected. An old pain that has grown larger in the dark.

**If someone tended your wound:**
You're receiving care and support from someone — or you're longing for it.

**If you tended someone else's wound:**
Your capacity to see another person's pain and contribute to their healing — a caring impulse.

**If the wound was healing in the dream:**
A positive process is underway. Something that's been open is closing.

**If you didn't know where the wound came from:**
An unconscious wound — something that hurts, but whose source you haven't yet identified.

## The wound as visible vulnerability

Wound symbolism is universal across human mythological thinking. The wounded hero is a recurring archetypal motif: Achilles at the heel, Siegfried between the shoulder blades, Amfortas with his unhealing wound in the story of Parsifal. All of them are men whose strength is near-absolute — except at one point. That point is the wound.

Jung used the phrase "the wounded self" for the psychic pain that arises during individuation — the encounter with the shadow, with suppressed parts of the self, with life's inevitable losses. Wounds in dreams are often markers of exactly these points of encounter.

Folk-medical tradition in Norway — as in most cultures — distinguishes between a wound that heals and a wound that "lives." A wound that "lives" is one that has taken on a spiritual or emotional charge and won't heal with physical treatment alone. The wound in a dream often belongs to this category. A [scar](/en/dreams/scar/) is what's left once this kind of wound finally closes.
