## What does it mean to dream of waves?

You are standing at the water's edge or out in it, and you watch it build. It has not
arrived yet. You have time to register that it is coming, but not enough time to get away.

It is that waiting that makes the wave dream distinctive. Where the
[ocean](/en/dreams/ocean/) is a state, the wave is an event — something with a beginning, a
point of impact and an end.

## What you see coming

### The waiting that is the whole dream

Most people remember the moment before the wave hit, not the impact itself. The dream dwells
on the warning, and that is a precise image for situations where you know what is coming
without being able to affect it.

### The wave that comes again

Waves come in sets. Many describe getting through one and then seeing that the next was
already on its way. That is a different image from a flood, which rises and stays. The wave
passes — and returns.

### The size people remember

Almost everyone describes the wave as larger than any they have seen in life. That says more
about the feeling than about the water, and the size is therefore seldom worth reading
literally.

## What you did with the wave

**If you ducked under it:**
The most common strategy, both in dreams and in water. Often described in periods when
someone has chosen to let something pass rather than meet it.

**If it hit you at full force:**
Can mirror something that arrived before you could prepare. Often remembered in the body
rather than in images.

**If you rode it:**
A strikingly positive variant. Using the force rather than resisting it is often described in
periods when something demanding went unexpectedly well.

**If the wave never arrived:**
Common, and often the most unsettling. A threat left permanently on its way, never resolving.

**If you watched from shore:**
Distance changes everything. Observing something overwhelming without being in it is an
entirely different dream from standing in the water.

## When the wave becomes more than that

If the wave grows into something covering the whole horizon, you are in a different dream —
that of the [tsunami](/en/dreams/tsunami/), where the catastrophe is the entire picture. The
wave in this article is something you can come through.

The dream also differs from [swimming](/en/dreams/swimming/), where you move yourself. Here
the water moves, and you respond to it.

## Sources and further reading

- Antti Revonsuo, "The reinterpretation of dreams" (2000) — threat simulation theory, relevant
  to the dream dwelling on the warning rather than on the impact.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — on water and feeling, relevant to why the
  wave is read as something arriving from outside.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis,
  relevant to the dream arriving when something predictable and unavoidable is approaching.
