## What does it mean to dream about a pig?

In the Chinese zodiac, the pig is the sign of abundance and prosperity, while in everyday English the word carries associations of mess or excess. The pig carries this same tension straight into dream symbolism: something abundant, vital, and quietly shameful all at once.

Interestingly, the pig is one of the most intelligent and curious animals we keep — a trait that rarely gets acknowledged in its popular symbolism.

## Abundance, intelligence, and cultural shame

### Abundance and indulgence

The most common reading is about abundance — food, pleasure, taking in more than necessary. A pig in a dream can mirror a period of excess: too much of a good thing, or a wish for more pleasure and satisfaction in life.

### Underrated intelligence

The pig is remarkably intelligent, yet it's treated as though it isn't. A pig in a dream can represent an intelligent, curious side of yourself that isn't getting the recognition it deserves.

### Something you're ashamed of

In Western culture, the pig carries a cultural association with shame — "filthy," dirty, unrestrained. Dreaming of a pig can mirror something you're keeping hidden, something you don't want to own, an impulse or need you experience as too crude or too rough.

## Lush, in the mud, or an aggressive appetite

If the pig was healthy and lush, the dream points toward abundance and vitality — something is thriving in the natural and the earthy. If it was lying in mud, that can draw on the old image of uncleanliness, but mud is also where growth happens: something that looks messy at first glance can turn out to be fertile. If there were piglets in the dream, it's usually about something new, vulnerable, and full of potential — a fresh start in its most tender form.

If the pig was aggressive, it may point toward a suppressed appetite or need demanding expression. And if you ate pork in the dream, you're taking in nourishment from something you may have felt ambivalent about.

## The golden bristles and what Jung would call shame

In Norse tradition, the pig is actually a noble animal — Freyr, the god of fertility, rode the boar Gullinbursti, said to outrun any horse. The residents of Valhalla ate the meat of the boar Sæhrímnir each evening and found it whole and fresh again the next morning. In the Norse imagination, the pig is life's abundance and regenerative power personified.

Jung would likely have seen the pig dream as an invitation to explore the sides of life we've learned to feel ashamed of — the physical, the appetite-driven, whatever doesn't fit polished norms but is nonetheless a natural part of being human.

## Sources and further reading

- Artemidorus of Daldis, *Oneirocritica* (c. 150–200 AD) — livestock and abundance as a dream motif, without the shame later ages attached to the animal.
- Carl Gustav Jung, *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — the shadow and the appetite: what we call "piggish" is often what we won't own in ourselves.
- G. William Domhoff, *The Scientific Study of Dreams* (2003) — the continuity hypothesis, and why the pig's condition colours the reading.
