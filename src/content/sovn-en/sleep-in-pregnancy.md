Pregnancy is one of the periods in life when sleep changes the most, and when it's needed the most. It's a frustrating paradox: just as the body is doing some of its biggest work, a good night's sleep is harder to come by than ever.

And the dreams? They become more intense, more bizarre and more memorable than most people have experienced since childhood.

## What happens to sleep during pregnancy

Pregnancy changes sleep through a combination of hormonal, physical and emotional factors, and these shift across the three trimesters.

### First trimester

Progesterone rises dramatically in the first trimester. It's a sleep-promoting hormone, which is why so many pregnant people feel intensely sleepy in the early months, often in the middle of the day, even after an apparently decent night. But progesterone also disrupts sleep architecture and produces more restless nights.

Frequent urination often starts here too. Nighttime nausea and breast tenderness are other common disruptors.

The typical pattern in the first trimester: a lot of daytime sleepiness, but fragmented, shallow sleep at night. Many people are nodding off by early evening and still feel tired.

### Second trimester

For many, the second trimester is the best sleep period of pregnancy. Nausea has usually eased, energy levels improve, and the belly isn't yet large enough to make sleeping positions impossible.

Still, new challenges appear: nighttime leg cramps (common and uncomfortable), restless legs, and heartburn are frequent complaints. Snoring often starts in the second trimester, as increased blood volume and swollen nasal passages narrow the airway.

### Third trimester

The third trimester often brings the hardest sleep of all. A growing belly makes comfortable positions difficult to find — lying on the back isn't recommended, since it puts pressure on the inferior vena cava and can reduce blood flow to the fetus. Side-lying is the recommended alternative, often with a pregnancy pillow between the knees.

Frequent bathroom trips, the baby's movements at night, pelvic and lower back pain, and emotional worries about birth and parenthood all contribute to fragmented sleep.

Research links poor sleep in the third trimester to longer labour and a higher risk of caesarean delivery — a finding that makes it especially worth taking sleep seriously during this stretch.

## The vivid dreams of pregnancy

One of the most consistent and dramatic sleep changes during pregnancy is how intense dreams become. Pregnant people report notably more vivid, bizarre and emotionally charged dreams than usual, and remember them far better on waking.

Research points to two mechanisms. Frequent waking — especially in the third trimester — means you're more often interrupted mid-REM sleep and recall the dream's content. The more often you wake from REM, the more of your dreams you remember.

Hormonal changes, particularly increased progesterone and oestrogen, also directly affect brain activity patterns during REM sleep.

Pregnancy dreams have recognisable themes: dreams about the baby (too small, too big, healthy or not), dreams about giving birth, dreams about losing something precious, dreams about an ex (a common phenomenon that rarely means what people fear it does), and bizarre scenarios that blend the baby's arrival with other areas of life.

These dreams are normal and are a sign the brain is processing one of the biggest life changes a person can go through. See the symbol page for [pregnant](/en/dreams/pregnant/) for more on what these dreams can mean.

## Practical tips for better sleep during pregnancy

**Sleeping position.** Side-lying is best — the left side in particular reduces pressure on the aorta and supports blood flow to the baby. A pregnancy pillow that supports the belly and sits between the knees makes side-sleeping far more comfortable.

**Heartburn.** Raise the head of the bed by a few centimetres (something under the mattress, not just extra pillows) and avoid eating in the last two to three hours before bed.

**Frequent urination.** Cut back on fluids in the last couple of hours before bed. Some people find that using the bathroom right before lying down, waiting a couple of minutes, and going again empties the bladder more thoroughly.

**Leg cramps.** Stretch your calf muscles before bed. Magnesium supplements — discussed with a midwife or doctor first — have reasonable evidence for reducing leg cramps in pregnancy.

**Emotional unease.** Write down worries and thoughts before bed, not to solve them, but to set them aside. A simple journal works well. A [dream journal](/en/guides/dream-journal/) that also captures evening thoughts and morning dreams can be especially useful during this period.

**Daytime rest.** A short nap in the middle of the day isn't laziness — it's necessary compensation. After the first trimester, lying flat on your back for long periods isn't recommended, so favour side-lying or a seated position.

## What to avoid

Sleep medication generally isn't recommended during pregnancy without talking to a doctor first. Alcohol as a sleep aid is absolutely off the table — there's no known safe amount of alcohol during pregnancy. Be careful with herbal teas too, since not all of them are considered safe in pregnancy.

## After the birth

Sleep changes again after birth, drastically. Newborns sleep for long stretches across the day, but in short bursts that aren't synchronised with the parents' rhythm. The first month is often one of the most sleep-deprived periods of an adult's life.

It's worth knowing: sleep deprivation after birth contributes to a higher risk of postpartum depression. Accepting help, sharing night shifts, and prioritising sleep over other things isn't selfish — it's medically necessary.

## The bottom line

Sleep during pregnancy is genuinely difficult, but there are things that help:

- First trimester: sleepy, but fragmented at night — progesterone is the driver
- Second trimester: often the best sleep of pregnancy, though leg cramps and heartburn appear
- Third trimester: the hardest stretch — belly, bathroom trips and worry all disrupt sleep
- Pregnancy dreams are intense and memorable, and reflect the brain's normal processing of a major life change
- Side-lying with a pregnancy pillow, limiting fluids in the evening, and short daytime rests are the most useful measures
